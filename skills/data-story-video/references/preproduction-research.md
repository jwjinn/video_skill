# Research before a new video

Use this workflow before scripting a **new topic, episode, or substantial recut**. For a narrow correction such as a palette or caption fix, reuse the existing research unless the topic, audience, or source freshness has changed. The user's request can limit or skip research.

## Two kinds of references

1. **Audience questions and current discussion:** What is the intended audience asking, debating, or trying to implement now? For LinkedIn, prioritize professional cases, implementation choices, failure conditions and verification. For Instagram, study approachable framing and short visual explanations separately. Select sources relevant to the actual topic; AI development is not the only possible subject.
2. **Visual storytelling:** How does an actual video or image explain the idea? Inspect its opening, scene timing, chart motion, explanation density, legend, transitions, and ending. Preserve the approved HIS01 foundation and adapt only elements that help this story.

Start with approximately the last 30–60 days relative to the research date. Expand the window for sparse or specialized subjects and record why. Older references can still be useful visual precedents; label them as such instead of calling them current trends. Prefer original posts, creators' own videos, and primary technical sources when checking technical claims.

## Inspect, then describe

Search across multiple independent authors and relevant formats; group repeated questions and contrasting views. Continue until the important candidate themes have adequate coverage. Do not equate a small convenience sample with the whole platform's trend, and do not fill a quota with unrelated sources. Report the sample's dates, authors, channels, coverage and limits. Engagement figures are optional dated observations, not proof that a technique caused popularity.

Actually open the original material. A search snippet or thumbnail is insufficient to describe a video's pacing. Record the exact watched segments or inspected image/page. If only the text, thumbnail, or a short segment is accessible, say so and limit the observation accordingly. Never invent the unseen structure, metrics, dates, or a creator's rationale.

For each useful reference, save:

|Field|Content|
|---|---|
|Identity|Stable ID, title, original URL, author, channel, format|
|Freshness|Publication date when available, access/research date, and unknown dates explicitly|
|Inspection|Text/image/video access level; video time ranges actually watched|
|Observation|What question is addressed; what the screen actually does|
|Application|Which episode/scene could use the technique, and why it helps|
|Evidence boundary|Which user-provided document/log/CSV supports our own story; missing support|

Summarize techniques in your own words. Reference a visual principle rather than copying the creator's wording, artwork, branding or footage into the deliverable.

## Connect interest to the user's actual evidence

Create an application table before drafting scenes:

|Observed audience question|Applicable user evidence|Our grounded insight|Visual technique / scene|Limit|
|---|---|---|---|---|

Select topics where the user's own records can substantiate a useful answer. A popular claim does not make it true for this project. Clearly separate external discussion, the user's documented history, and the editor's inference. If the required record is absent, change the angle or mark the gap; do not invent an experience to match a trend.

Keep one coherent question per video. This research should help choose and explain that question, not reintroduce fragmented topics or force a new visual style on every episode. Proceed from the recorded selection into the script without introducing a routine approval gate.

## Save the work for reuse

Use `00-사전리서치/` at the collection or episode level:

- `트렌드-조사.md`: question clusters, freshness window, scope, observations, uncertainties and candidate angles.
- `레퍼런스-목록.json`: the reference fields above, with stable IDs.
- `적용-계획.md`: selected references mapped to evidence and proposed scenes; after rendering, record what was actually applied and where.

For a shared collection-level research folder, include each episode's relevant observations, source attribution, selected techniques and application reasons **inside its self-contained ID.md**, not just links to that folder. The whole article need not be copied. Keep the reusable source index so later sessions can revisit it, and recheck freshness before calling an old sample current.

If browsing or the original media is unavailable, record the limitation and use identifiable supplied references as a provisional basis. Do not claim that fresh trend research was completed. Research guides topic selection and presentation; project logs and measurements establish the video's historical claims.
